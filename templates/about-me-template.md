# About Me: template

The single highest-leverage document in your project. It's what makes everything the AI writes sound like you and not like generic AI copy. Spend 20 honest minutes on it. It pays back on every draft.

Most people write this doc too politely. The value is in the honest parts: what you actually want, what you won't do, how you actually write.

## How to build it

Two ways. The second is easier if writing about yourself makes you freeze.

**Option A: fill it in yourself.** Use the structure below. Write in first person, plain language.

**Option B: have the AI interview you.** Paste this:

```
I'm starting a job search and I need an About Me document. Interview me, one question at a time, and wait for each answer. Push back on vague answers. If I say something generic like "I'm a strong communicator," ask what specifically I did that proves it.

Cover, in this order: my career arc, what I'm known for, my point of view on my craft, my point of view on AI in my work, what I want next, my deal-breakers, and how I write when I'm being myself.

After all questions, write the document in my own words and phrasing, not yours. Keep my rough edges. Do not polish me into a brochure. Flag any place where you had to guess.
```

## The structure

```markdown
# About Me: [Your name]

## Career arc in 200 words
Where you started, the through-line, what you've gotten better at, where you
are now. First person. A story, not a summary.

## What I'm known for
3 to 5 bullets. Specific skills, signature moves, the things people come to
you for. "I turn messy spreadsheets into a decision a VP can make in five
minutes" beats "strong analytical skills."

## My point of view on [your craft]
2 or 3 sentences. What do you believe about doing this work well that not
everyone agrees with? Interviewers will probe this.

## My point of view on AI in my work
2 or 3 sentences. You will be asked. Have a real answer, not a hedge.

## What I want next
The role, the team, the kind of work, the kind of company. Be specific.
"A senior lifecycle role at a product-led company where I own onboarding
end to end" beats "the next step in my career."

## Deal-breakers
Be honest with yourself. Full return to office? Certain industries? A
salary floor? Travel? Reporting into a particular function? List them.
The AI uses this to filter roles and to stop suggesting things you'd never take.

## Voice notes
How you write when you're being yourself. Sentence length. Humor or none.
Whether you use dashes. Reading level. Words you'd never use.
(This section is the most valuable one. If you build a full voice profile,
see voice-profile-guide.md, and link to it here.)

## Personal context
Anything that shapes the search: family, health, timeline, finances, how
long you can be selective. The AI uses it to set realistic advice.

## Notes to the AI
Standing instructions specific to you. Example: "I tend to undersell.
Push me to lead with numbers." or "Don't suggest I freelance. I've tried it."
```

## Test it

Once it's in your project along with your resume, start a fresh chat and paste:

```
Sanity check. Based on everything in the project, give me a 4-sentence positioning statement for my search. Then tell me one thing my materials are missing that would make my positioning sharper.
```

If the answer sounds like you and the gap it flags rings true, the project is set up well. If it sounds generic, your About Me is too vague. Tighten the "known for" and "voice notes" sections and add two or three examples of writing you think sounds like you.

## Troubleshooting

- **Outputs sound generic.** Tighten Voice Notes. Add real writing samples.
- **The AI invents accomplishments.** Add to your project instructions: "Never fabricate accomplishments. If you don't have evidence for something in the project files, ask me before including it." Then build the story blocks, so there's evidence to draw from.
- **The project feels bloated.** You've added things you don't need. Project files should be reference material you'd want the AI to consult. Trim.
