# Outreach templates

Starting shapes for the messages you'll send most. Adapt, don't paste. Run every one through the cleanup pass (`skills/cleanup-pass.md`) before it goes out.

Defaults across all of them: plain text, no call to action beyond leaving the door open, no "I'd love to pick your brain," no "circling back," no "hope this finds you well," no filler thanks. If you have nothing specific to say about the person, don't fake it. Be direct.

---

## Follow-up messages (used by `skills/draft-followup.md`)

Three cases, split by who's on the other end. That's what changes the message, not how long it's been quiet.

### Case 1: after a conversation, then silence

A screen or interview happened. The decision hasn't come. The recipient is the person you actually spoke to.

```
Subject: Following up on [ROLE]

Hi [NAME],

Wanted to check in on [ROLE]. We spoke [TIMEFRAME] and I haven't heard
anything since. If the timeline's shifted or you need anything else from me,
let me know.

[Your first name]
```

### Case 2: a referral or warm connection went quiet

The application went through someone you know. The recipient is that person, not the company. The relationship matters more than this one application.

```
Subject: Checking in on [COMPANY]

Hi [NAME],

Wanted to check in on the [ROLE] referral. Haven't heard anything since
[WHEN/WHAT HAPPENED]. No pressure if it's gone quiet on their end, just
didn't want to leave you without an update from my side.

[Your first name]
```

### Case 3: recruiter silence

Recruiters screen for placeability, not passion for your vertical. Keep it short and about availability.

```
Subject: Checking in on [ROLE]

Hi [NAME],

Following up on [ROLE] at [COMPANY]. Still interested and available if it's
still moving. Let me know where things stand.

[Your first name]
```

### Not covered

A cold application with no real contact usually has nowhere to follow up. The draft-followup skill flags those instead of inventing a recipient.

**Two follow-ups per application, maximum.** A third unanswered follow-up costs more than it can return.

## Reaching out to someone at a company you're applying to

75 to 150 words. Use `workflows/linkedin-outreach.md` for the full rules. The shape:

```
Hi [NAME],

[One specific, real reason you're writing to this person. A project of theirs,
a post, a shared employer. If you don't have one, skip this line.]

I'm applying for [ROLE] at [COMPANY]. [One sentence on why the work at
[COMPANY] fits what you've done, using a fact from your story blocks.]

[Optional, only if true: one narrow question they can answer in one line.]

[Your first name]
```

An older template asked "Is [company] in a hiring freeze right now?" and whether the role had anything worth knowing before applying. That kind of narrow, answerable question works better than "can I pick your brain."

## After applying through an easy-apply button, to a recruiter or agency

For recruiting agencies and staffing firms, where placement across multiple clients is the point.

```
Hi [NAME],

I just applied to the [ROLE] position on LinkedIn: [JOB URL]

Since the listing was easy-apply, I wanted to send a link to my work in case it's
useful in evaluating me: [PORTFOLIO OR PROFILE LINK]

I'd like to connect and see whether my background fits other roles your
clients have open. I'm available for [full-time / contract / freelance].

[Your first name]
```

## A generator prompt for cold messages

For a person you don't know at all. From the playbook.

```
I want to reach out to [name], [title] at [company]. I do NOT want to pick their brain or ask vague favors.

What I know about them: [LinkedIn snippets, recent posts, work I admire, mutual connections]
My relevant background, in 2 or 3 sentences: [text]

Draft three versions, 75 to 100 words each:
1. Admiring: references a specific piece of their work.
2. Offering value: something useful I noticed, made, or can share.
3. Specific question: one narrow, easy-to-answer thing.

Ban: "I'd love to pick your brain." "Circling back." "Hope this finds you well." Any claim about me not in my story blocks.
```
