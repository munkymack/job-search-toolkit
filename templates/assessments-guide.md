# Using assessments as search inputs

Optional. A way to get an outside read on what you want and how you work, in a form you can feed into your strategy and story blocks.

## An example of how it was used

One worked example used four instruments as inputs to a strategy doc:

- A Big Five personality test (IPIP-NEO-120 style)
- HEXACO-PI-R, a six-factor personality inventory
- CareerOneStop's Work Values Matcher (free, from the US Department of Labor)
- A motivation instrument (Your Self Atlas) built around what drives you

The result files were uploaded to an AI, which was asked to find where all four agreed. The overlap set the weights in a decision framework (`search-strategy-template.md`). No result files are included. Take your own.

## Be honest about what these are

Personality tests are weak evidence. Treat the results as prompts for reflection, not verdicts about who you are. Their real value is in two places:

1. **Where several instruments agree.** One test saying you're extraverted is a data point. Four different tests pointing the same way is a pattern worth building a search around.
2. **Where they disagree with each other or with you.** If two instruments score the same trait differently, reconciling them makes you name your actual weak spots. And when a tool's summary blurb says something that doesn't match your lived experience, and your own detailed scores don't support it either, say so and have the read corrected. **You outrank the report.**

## How to use them

1. Take one or more assessments. Save the result files (PDF or text).
2. Upload them to a fresh chat, not your main project, if you'd rather keep them out of the standing context.
3. Run:

```
Here are my assessment results. Do not summarize each one. I want three things.

1. Where do the instruments agree? Cite the specific scores, not the labels.
2. Where do they disagree, and what's the most plausible reconciliation?
3. For each conclusion, tell me how much weight it can bear. Flag anything that's a stretch from the data.

Then list what this implies for my search: what kind of role, team, and daily work would feed me, and what would drain me. Separate what the data supports from what you're inferring.

I will correct anything that doesn't match my experience. My account outranks the report.
```

4. Correct it. Say what's wrong. Have it revise.
5. Move the survivors into `search-strategy.md` as a dated "assessment synthesis" section. Record your corrections there too.

## Where it feeds

- **Story blocks:** skills and traits that recur become candidates for positioning blocks, if you have evidence for them.
- **Decision framework:** criteria weights (why team contact is a 5, why variety is a 4).
- **Deal-breakers in About Me:** drains you identified.

## When to skip it

If it would take you a weekend of hand-wringing and you already know what you want. Or if you'd end up treating the results as gospel. It's not a required step. It's a way to break a tie when your own read is muddy.
