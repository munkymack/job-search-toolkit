# Writing rules and craft rules: starter files

Two short files for your project. Both are living documents. `skills/capture-lessons.md` grows them.

- **`writing-rules.md`** is voice: how a sentence sounds. Word choice, rhythm, AI tells.
- **`craft-rules.md`** is structure: how a document is put together. Framing, what claims go where.

Keep them short and declarative. One line per rule. No explaining why a rule works. If a rule needs a paragraph of justification, it isn't a rule yet.

Everything below is a starter list. Edit hard. Cut what isn't yours. A rule you don't believe in will make your output sound like someone else.

---

## writing-rules.md (starter)

```markdown
# Writing voice and craft

Applies to anything written in my name: cover letters, resumes, outreach, emails.
Read before drafting.

## AI tells: cut on sight
- "Not just X, but Y."
- Negative-parallelism closers: "X, they don't replace it." Sounds punchy, says nothing new. Cut the second clause or the whole line.
- Self-graded significance tags on my own work ("is work a firm rarely allows itself"). State what I did. Skip the verdict on how impressive it is.
- "X is doing A. The real question is B."
- "Small thing on paper, but actually significant."
- Aspirational closers ("that's the work I want to do").
- Sentences that explain why their own argument works.
- Stock industry phrases that sound like voice but are filler. Replace with the literal claim.
- Hedge words that read like form letters. "I'd welcome being kept on file" becomes "keep me on file."
- Frame-then-list meta commentary ("a pattern running through it:", "what ties this together is:"). State the specifics.
- Self-graded opening line ("X is the closest match"). Start with the concrete fact.
- Three clauses stitched with commas, or two fragment lists in a row. Vary length and shape.
- [YOUR CALL: em dashes. Some writers ban them in anything a person reads. Decide yours, then state it here.]

## Voice
- Read it out loud. If no human would say it, rewrite it.
- Name observable things ("gets reposted"), not concepts ("earns loyalty").
- The formula sneaks in during the back half of paragraphs. Watch it.
- Don't parrot the JD's language. Facts show the match.
- Summary = two sentences about who I am, not a JD remix.
- A vague claim everyone makes gets cut or backed. "Detail-oriented" becomes the specific thing underneath.
- Let evidence prove the claim. Shown beats told.
- Don't end a bullet or sentence on a stray preposition ("managed vendors for"). Restructure to close on the noun.
- Cut hollow verb phrasing ("had to do the work across"). Use the direct verb.
- Name the real thing, not the abstraction. If I can't name what it actually is, I don't understand it well enough to write about it.

## Precision and structure
- Use real names. "Acme Sales Academy," not "web-based learning destination."
- Adjacent bullets imply a link. Don't seat a tooling claim beside a results claim unless the causation is real.
- Two projects sharing a paragraph: name each one in its own sentence. If the second stays unnamed, it reads as more evidence for the first.

## Banned vocabulary
passionate, results-driven, dynamic, leverage, utilize, synergy, unlock, "fast-paced world" openers, powerful, seamless, robust, innovative, rule-of-three padding.
[ADD YOUR OWN.]

## Cover letter banned lines
"Excited to apply." "Passionate [role] with X years." "Came across your posting." "I believe I'd be a great fit." "Thank you for considering."

## Formatting
Skip header, bullet, and bold spam. Skip bullets when prose reads better. Skip tables for non-comparison data. Bold only for real emphasis.
```

## craft-rules.md (starter)

```markdown
# Craft rules

Act on these. Don't re-derive them.

## Reading a job description
- Find the unstated brief. "What you'll need" is the real filter.
- A repeated word is the real brief. "High-volume" twice means throughput, not concept. Read what they say most, not first.
- Odd requirements reveal the real hire. A creator track record on a content JD means they want an audience builder.
- A buried bullet is low priority. "Develop headlines" listed under proofreading means an execution job, not an ideas job.
- Identify the most likely rejection reason. Decide: preempt it, or ignore it.

## Framing
- Match claims to story blocks exactly. Don't inflate partial contribution into full ownership. "Created some, edited others" is not "built the system." Watch scope words: system, owns, every, all.
- Resume and cover letter agree on what I did.
- Match positioning to the role's level. Don't surface leadership blocks for a role that wants a hands-on specialist.
- Internal-facing work gets called internal-facing. Let the real value do the work.
- Don't claim keywords I can't back.
- Lead with the reader's problem, not what I'm selling.

## Cover letters
- 200 words or fewer for most roles. About 300 in three paragraphs when the role warrants it. Never more than three paragraphs.
- Lead with their problem, then how I solve it.
- Story, not stat dump. Numbers support the story. They don't drive it.
- Never mention being between jobs. It's not their business.

## Outreach
- 75 to 150 words.
- Open specifically (a post, a project) only if there's something real. Otherwise be direct. No forced "I saw your post."
- No call to action by default. Open the door. Let them decide.
```

## How to install

1. Save each block as its own markdown file.
2. Edit until every line is one you'd defend.
3. Upload both to your project.
4. Mention them by name in your project instructions (see your setup doc).
5. For the handful of rules that must never lapse, also put them in the project instructions themselves. Uploaded files are searched, not guaranteed to be read every turn. Instructions are always on.
