# Search strategy: template

The strategy document is where you write down what you're aiming at, what you believe about yourself, and what you're going to watch for. Most job seekers never do it. The ones who do drift less.

A mature version of this document runs to a few hundred lines and gets updated as the search teaches you things. Yours can start at one page.

The hard part isn't the schedule. It's the honesty. Most people won't write "I'm not actually ready for the senior title" or "my resume is undermining me." Doing so is the difference between a search that drifts and one that converges.

## The structure

```markdown
# Search strategy and context

## The diagnosis
What's actually going wrong or what needs to change. Name the pain points
(ghosting, no responses, can't find good roles, dreading tailoring) and the
root cause of each. A table works: pain point / root cause / antidote.

## Strategic decisions made
Decisions that are settled, so you stop relitigating them. Example: "Two
tracks in parallel: [role A] and [role B], positioned separately." Date them.

## Honest read on current materials
### Strengths
### Gaps to address
### Open strategic question
The one big decision you haven't made, stated plainly.

## Targets and goals
- Roles (primary, secondary, title flexibility)
- Industries
- Flag-don't-auto-exclude list: sectors you'd look at case by case
- Where you want to be in two years

## Positioning: lead claim
One sentence: the claim your whole search leads with. Then the proof: which
story blocks back it.

## Positioning hypotheses (to test)
3 to 5 working claims about what makes you distinctive. Label them
hypotheses. Interview feedback and response data will confirm or kill them.

## What to watch for
Signal / what it might mean / what to do. Table.

## Tools and workflow stack
What you use for what. Where the canonical files live. Backups.

## Constraints
Salary floor (or "no floor, fit outranks cash"), location, timeline,
contract willingness, deal-breakers. Date them and update when they change.

## Things this document should get updated with
A checklist of events that should trigger an update: story blocks done,
resume revised, first batch of responses, interview feedback.
```

## "What to watch for": starter table

This one transfers to almost any field. Adjust the specifics.

| Signal | What it might mean | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Ambitious-level applications ghosted, mid-level ones get responses | Materials read one level lower than your target | Tighten the positioning or shift the primary aim |
| Interviewers keep asking "have you led a team?" | The leadership story isn't on the page | Restructure the resume and add an explicit leadership block |
| Outreach gets responses but applications don't | Networks outperform boards for you | Put more weekly time into outreach |
| Applications get responses, but interviews stall at round two | Portfolio or interview answers aren't landing | Portfolio gut-check and interview rehearsal (`prompts-library.md`) |
| No responses anywhere for three or more weeks | The materials are the bottleneck, not the channel | Stop applying. Revise the materials. Restart. |

## The optional heavy version: path map and decision framework

Do this once you have real evidence, roughly a few months in. It's worth doing after your first few weeks, not before.

**Step 1: List candidate paths.** Four or five archetypes, not specific jobs. Example shape: in-house individual contributor, agency or consultancy on a leadership track, in-house player-coach, function-shift role, and a bridge contract as a deliberate holding pattern.

**Step 2: Two gates first (pass/fail, before any scoring).** Pick the two things that are non-negotiable for you and test every path against them. Examples: "at least about 40% hands-on work in my craft" and "location." Fail either and the path is out. Nothing below matters if a gate fails.

**Step 3: Criteria and weights.** List 6 to 8 criteria. Weight each from 1 to 5 by how much it matters *to you*, and write one line on why. Salary is deliberately optional: if you have no firm floor and rank fit above cash, leave it off. If you do, score it. Say which either way.

**Step 4: Score each path 1 to 5 on each criterion.** Multiply by weight. Sum. Rank.

**Step 5: Read it honestly.** Note that the scorecard scores archetypes, not roles. A specific role can swing several criteria hard. Re-score any live opportunity on those lines before trusting the number. Note whose inputs the weights came from. If they're the AI's read of your inputs, not your own scoring, say so, and re-run with your own before treating the numbers as settled.

**Step 6: Write the decision rules that fall out.** For example: "Lead with paths 2 and 3. Keep path 1 as a standing fallback but screen every one for team size. Treat path 4 as opportunistic. Use path 5 tactically and time-box it."

Prompt to run it:

```
Using my About Me, story blocks, assessment results (if any), and constraints, help me build a decision framework.

1. Propose 4 or 5 candidate paths as archetypes, not specific jobs.
2. Ask me for my two non-negotiable gates. Do not choose them for me.
3. Propose 6 to 8 criteria, and ask me to set the weight for each. Show me the criteria first and wait.
4. Score each path. Show your reasoning for any score that isn't obvious.
5. State plainly where the scores come from my inputs and where they're your inference.
6. Write the decision rules that fall out. Then tell me what would change the ranking.
```

## A thing worth doing at the end

Add a "Notes for future me" section: what you'd tell someone else running this search. Common ones: the standing instructions matter more than the individual prompts, the story block library is one-time work that pays back, and the day most people skip (network outreach) is the day with the highest return. Write your own after a month.
